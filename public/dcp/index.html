Questions

What is a DCP?

A DCP (Digital Cinema Package) is the set of files cinema servers play: JPEG 2000 picture and PCM audio wrapped in MXF, plus XML files that describe the playlist. Festivals that screen in cinemas ask for a DCP instead of an MP4 or ProRes file.

How much does a DCP cost?

A short film up to 15 minutes is $149 and up to 40 minutes is $249, validation and download delivery included. Subtitles are $49 and 24-hour rush is $79. Features are quoted.

Can I convert an MP4 to a DCP?

Yes, if the MP4 is a high-bitrate export at the film's native resolution and frame rate. ProRes, DNxHR or an image sequence gives a better result. We check the file before we start and tell you if it needs a new export.

Can I make a DCP myself with DCP-o-matic?

Yes. DCP-o-matic is free and good. Most rejected DIY DCPs fail on the details: frame rate, color conversion, audio channel mapping, subtitle timing, naming or drive format.

Pay for one when the deadline is close or a failed screening would cost more than the service.

How long does it take?

3 business days from the moment we have your file, or 24 hours with rush.

How big is a DCP file?

Roughly 1 to 2 GB per minute at 2K, so a 15-minute short is about 15 to 30 GB.

Do I need a SMPTE or an Interop DCP?

Most festivals now ask for SMPTE; some older cinema servers still need Interop. Tell us the festival and we follow its technical requirements.

How do I get the DCP to the festival?

You get a download link you can forward to the festival or upload to its portal. If the festival wants a drive, the files come ready to copy to an ext3-formatted drive, the format cinema servers read.
